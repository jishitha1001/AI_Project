RULE OF THIRDS

The Rule of Thirds divides an image into nine equal sections using two horizontal and two vertical lines. Placing the main subject near the intersections or along these lines can make the composition more balanced and interesting.

LEADING LINES

Leading lines are lines in a photograph that guide the viewer's eyes toward the main subject. Roads, railings, buildings, bridges, and pathways can be used as leading lines.

NEGATIVE SPACE

Negative space is the empty area around the main subject. Leaving enough empty space can make the subject stand out and create a clean and simple composition.

LIGHTING

Good lighting is important for photography. Soft natural light is often useful for portraits. Avoid very harsh light when possible because it can create strong shadows.

EXPOSURE

Exposure controls how bright or dark a photograph appears. If an image is too dark, increasing exposure can help. If an image is too bright, reducing exposure can preserve details.

BRIGHTNESS

Brightness affects the overall lightness of an image. A dark photograph may need more brightness, while an overly bright photograph may need less brightness.

CONTRAST

Contrast is the difference between the dark and bright areas of an image. Low contrast can make an image look flat, while excessive contrast can remove details from shadows and highlights.

PORTRAIT PHOTOGRAPHY

For portraits, the subject should usually be clearly separated from the background. A simple background and good lighting can help the person stand out.

LANDSCAPE PHOTOGRAPHY

Landscape photographs can benefit from the Rule of Thirds, leading lines, foreground elements, and a clear subject. Including foreground, middle ground, and background can add depth.

BACKGROUND

A distracting background can take attention away from the subject. Before taking a photograph, check the area behind the subject and try to remove unnecessary distractions.

FOCUS

The main subject should be in focus. For portraits, focusing on the eyes usually helps create a stronger image.

COLOR

Colors can affect the mood of a photograph. Complementary colors can create contrast, while similar colors can create a calm and consistent appearance.

FRAMING

Natural or architectural elements such as windows, doors, branches, and arches can be used to frame the subject and draw attention to it.

DEPTH

Adding foreground, middle ground, and background elements can make a photograph appear more three-dimensional.

SIMPLICITY

Avoid including too many unnecessary elements. A simple composition can make the main subject easier to understand.

STREET PHOTOGRAPHY

Street photography often benefits from interesting light, strong compositions, leading lines, and capturing genuine moments. Look for interesting interactions and patterns.

ARCHITECTURE PHOTOGRAPHY

Architecture photography can benefit from symmetry, leading lines, geometric shapes, and careful perspective. Straight lines and balanced framing can improve the photograph.
